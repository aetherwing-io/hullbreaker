## 3. Judged at true size, not at 128px

Every candidate went through `view.mjs` at the size it will really have:
**24 runs** — 16 glyphs at `--tiles 1.045` (0.55 × the shipped `GLYPH_GAIN` 1.9 =
**18.2px**, the face the game actually draws today), 4 letters additionally at
`--tiles 0.55` (**9.6px**, the uncompensated control the checkpoint entry cites),
and the 2 HUD chips at `--height-px 32` and `44`. Every run reported the
expected pixel height against the 29.6px RIG bar. Six representative frames are
committed under `viewer/`; the rest are in gitignored `tools/assets/runs/`.

The images actually compared to reach the statements below are the seven sheets
in this directory, plus those viewer frames.

### The number the checkpoint entry predates

The queue entry says 9.6px and "the letter survives as a smudge". That was true
when T-015 measured it. **The shipped face is now 18.2px with 13.1px of letter
ink**, because the T-003 legibility pass scales the mesh by `GLYPH_GAIN` = 1.9
(`src/render/legibility.js`, asserted `tools/pathcheck.mjs:6084-6088` against
`LEGIBLE_PX_FLOOR` 12). Both rows are in every sheet; the row to judge is 18.2px.

### Direction A is already at its ceiling — this is the load-bearing finding

"Scale the glyph up" is entry 7's sanctioned fix and it is **spent**:

- `SHARE.glyph` is 1, so `GLYPH_GAIN` equals the FAR pull-back exactly. Pushing
  it past 1 breaks `tools/pathcheck.mjs:6089-6094` ("a compensated glyph lands at
  the SAME screen size at every view — restoring near, never overshooting it")
  and contradicts `legibility.js`'s own stated rule.
- The other lever is `CONFIG.capsules.size`, a **sim** constant asserted
  unchanged at `:6100`, which changes the pickup's world footprint.
- Containment headroom if the operator does decide to spend it: the shipped
  assertion (`CAPS.size * gain / 2 <= pickupRadius`) permits gain **3.4545** =
  33.1px; the corner-honest reading (the drawn face's *diagonal* inside the catch
  circle, `0.55·g·√2/2 ≤ 0.95`) permits **2.443** = 23.4px. Shipped: 1.9.

So more scale is a decision entry plus an assertion rewrite, not a number.

## 4. Measurements

All from the committed PNGs, box-downsampled to the true pixel height and
composited on the named backdrop. Luminance is Rec.709 on sRGB bytes.

**Discriminability — mean |ΔLuminance| per pixel between candidates in a set,
at the shipped 18.2px face, on the teal backdrop.** Higher = more different.

| Direction | worst pair | mean | best pair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A letters (S/L/H/F) | **12.3** (S/H) | 15.8 | 20.9 (L/F) |
| C ink marks | **18.6** (fan/wedge) | 24.8 | 29.5 (bar/ring) |
| D lit marks | **21.1** (fan/wedge) | 28.1 | 33.4 (bar/ring) |

Scaling barely moves them (A's worst pair: 12.1 at 9.6px → 12.4 at 33.1px).
Letters differ over a small share of the face, so **size is not the variable that
separates them** — which is the measured reason direction A cannot be fixed by
more of itself.

**Object-vs-background separation at 18.2px** (mean asset luminance minus
backdrop luminance):

| Candidate | vs teal `#143238` | vs lit deck `#c8834a` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A `capsule-plate-h` | +29.1 | −68.4 |
| C `capsule-mark-flame` | +32.7 | −64.8 |
| D `capsule-lit-flame` | **+89.4** | **−8.1** |
| D `capsule-lit-homing` | +92.1 | −5.4 |
| control `capsule-letter-h` | +63.5 | −34.0 |
| C `capsule-mark-mod` (amber face) | +91.7 | **−5.8** |

D is the only direction that produces pixels above luminance 200 (the
look-direction audit measured **0.0%** of playfield pixels above 200 in fifteen
gameplay captures) — and the only one that nearly disappears as a *field*
against the lit rust deck, which `src/render/palette.js` keeps as "the brightest
large surface in every mode". Neither polarity wins on every surface a capsule
appears on. The amber mod face has that problem already, independent of any
candidate here.

**An authored hex is not the pixel the game draws.** From the committed capture
`artifacts/look-v1/z04-capsule-letters-4x.png`: the shipped capsule face is
`PAL.capsule` `#ff4fd8` (L 126.3, CIELCh chroma ~76) on an unlit
`MeshBasicMaterial`, and it lands on screen at `rgb(226,167,214)` — **L 182.7,
chroma 33.2**. The ink letter `#14181e` (L 23.6) lands at `rgb(90,88,107)`, L
89.5. Plate-vs-letter contrast survives almost intact (102.7 → 93.2). Two known
transforms sit in between — ACES tone mapping, and the `CanvasTexture` that never
sets `colorSpace` (`src/render/capsules.js:121`) — and this measurement does not
apportion blame between them. The practical consequence for every candidate here:
**this pipeline's value relationships transfer to the screen; its hues and
absolute levels do not.** Recorded as limitation 11 in `tools/assets/README.md`.

## 5. Tooling added (dev-only, zero game effect)

`tools/assets/sheet.mjs` + `sheet.html` — the multi-asset companion to
`view.mjs`/`viewer.html`. The viewer can only answer "does *this* asset read at
this size"; a direction decision needs several candidates at one true size in one
image, which is what every sheet in this directory is. Same static server, same
Chrome, same scale arithmetic; `--px` rows bypass the viewport entirely, `--bg`
adds the three real backdrops (`teal` = `CONCEPT.bg`, `deck` = the lit rust deck,
`limb` = the six-face haze). Documented in `tools/assets/README.md` (tools table,
its own section, files list). Imports nothing from `src/`; not referenced by
`index.html`.

One scar worth keeping visible: the first version decoded the `spec` query
parameter twice (`URLSearchParams.get()` already decodes), so any label
containing a literal `%` threw and the whole sheet failed to build — found by a
footnote that read "29-34% of the frame". Fixed, with the reason in the file.

## 6. Limitations — read before treating any of this as proof

1. **The viewer and the sheet are flat composites.** No fog, perspective,
   lighting, mipmapping or tone mapping. They answer "does this read at this
   size", never "does this look right in the scene". Section 4's tone-mapping
   measurement is the size of the gap for one known case.
2. **The pixel metrics are not a human test.** Mean |ΔL| measures how different
   two drawings are, not whether a 9-year-old can name them at speed while
   dodging. No candidate was tested in motion, at a bend, behind fog, under a
   hit-flash, or with anything else on screen.
3. **Nothing here was seen in the game.** These are staged files; getting one
   into the render layer is a separate change that this lane deliberately did not
   make.
4. **The pipeline's 8-role palette folds amber into `warm-white`.** The mod
   capsule `#ffd75e` and the hound's WARN telegraph both classify as
   `warm-white`, so a green palette check **cannot** tell "mod capsule amber"
   from "muzzle warm-white" — relevant to direction D, whose whole cost is which
   role owns highlights. Noted, not changed: the role table is measured and
   shared, and re-cutting it is not this task's call.
5. **The `RG` letterforms are crude slab approximations** drawn as rectangles.
   The finding they support — a two-character label is a smudge at every legal
   size — does not depend on their quality, and a real font's thinner strokes
   would read worse, not better.
6. **PNG bytes are only reproducible against the same Chrome build** (the
   pipeline's existing limitation 5).
7. **Five symbols, not eight.** The shipped drop table is 4 weapons + 4 mods;
   directions C and D are shown with four weapon marks and one mod mark. The
   eighth topology is harder than the fourth, and that cost is stated in the
   packet rather than hidden by not drawing it.
